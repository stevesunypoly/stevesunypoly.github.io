The University of Maine in the Navatek story

Drawn from ProPublica, “The FBI Anti-Corruption Squad Was Circling Susan Collins — Until Trump Got in the Way,” September 22, 2026.

The University of Maine appears in the ProPublica story as Navatek’s Maine anchor. Under what employees called “the method,” Navatek concocted a research project with a university in a targeted appropriator’s home state — here, Collins on Defense Appropriations. UMaine supplied the credibility and the facilities, most visibly the giant polymer printer that produced 3Dirigo, the world’s largest 3D-printed boat, unveiled with a Guinness representative and a press conference.

The connective tissue was Glen Mandigo, the lobbyist Navatek hired to work Collins, who also lobbied for the University of Maine — a consultant positioned to broker exactly this kind of company-university pairing. Kao told the FBI that Mandigo asked how much funding Navatek wanted and how much Kao would support Collins. Mandigo denies any pay-to-play role. Collins’s office says calling him close to them is wholly inaccurate.

ProPublica makes no allegation against UMaine, which didn’t respond to its questions. But former employees said the Navy never wanted the boats.
